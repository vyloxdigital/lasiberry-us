The Problem

Why Modern Life Is Damaging Your Eyes — And How Lasiberry Defends Them

The human eye was not engineered for 10+ hours of daily blue light exposure from LED screens, smartphones, and energy-efficient lighting. Blue light penetrates deeper into the eye than other visible wavelengths — reaching the retina directly — and generates free radicals that gradually damage the light-sensitive photoreceptor cells that enable sharp, clear vision.

The National Eye Institute (NEI) identifies oxidative stress as a primary driver of age-related macular degeneration — the leading cause of vision loss in Americans over 50. Modern screen-heavy lifestyles accelerate this oxidative burden on the macula decades earlier than previous generations experienced.

✗ Digital eye strain — burning, itching, blurring after hours on screens
✗ Blue light retinal stress — daily LED and screen blue light reaching photoreceptors
✗ Reduced night vision — glare, halos around lights, diminished low-light clarity
✗ Macular pigment thinning — natural protective eye pigment decreases with age and poor diet
✗ Contrast sensitivity loss — colors appear less vivid, fine detail harder to resolve

The Vision Formula

Key Ingredients Inside Every Lasiberry Capsule

Lasiberry combines evidence-informed eye nutrients with powerful berry antioxidants — each ingredient chosen for its documented contribution to blue light defense, retinal protection, or visual function.

Ingredient 01
Bilberry Extract
Night Vision & Retinal Antioxidant

Bilberry (Vaccinium myrtillus) is the cornerstone of Lasiberry’s formula. Its anthocyanins — the deep-blue pigments that give bilberries their color — have one of the highest antioxidant capacities of any known plant compound. Historically used by WWII RAF pilots to sharpen night vision, bilberry anthocyanins concentrate in eye tissue and support retinal microcirculation, rod photoreceptor function, and protection against light-induced oxidative stress. Research on NIH PubMed extensively documents bilberry’s retinal benefits.

Lutein & Zeaxanthin
Ingredient 02
Macular Pigment & Blue Light Filter

Lutein and zeaxanthin are the only dietary carotenoids that accumulate specifically in the macular pigment of the human eye. This pigment acts as a natural internal sunscreen, filtering blue and UV light before it reaches the sensitive photoreceptor layer. The National Eye Institute’s AREDS2 study identified lutein and zeaxanthin as the primary nutrients for protecting macular health in aging adults.

Elderberry Extract
Ingredient 03
Antioxidant & Immune Eye Defense

Elderberry (Sambucus nigra) provides a rich source of anthocyanins and flavonoids that support immune defense in eye tissue and contribute to the formula’s antioxidant depth. Its polyphenols help neutralize free radicals generated by blue light exposure in both the aqueous humor (eye fluid) and the vitreous body, protecting multiple compartments of the eye simultaneously. See elderberry research at NIH NLM.

Vitamin A (Beta-Carotene)
Ingredient 04
Rhodopsin & Night Vision Support

Vitamin A is structurally essential to rhodopsin — the light-sensitive protein in rod photoreceptors that enables vision in low-light conditions. Without adequate Vitamin A, the eye cannot regenerate rhodopsin between light exposures, directly impairing night vision and dim-light visual adaptation. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements lists Vitamin A deficiency as the leading preventable cause of blindness worldwide.

Vitamin C & Vitamin E
Ingredient 05
Lens & Aqueous Humor Antioxidants

The eye’s lens and aqueous humor (the fluid filling the front chamber) are rich in Vitamin C — one of the highest concentrations of Vitamin C in any body tissue. This Vitamin C protects the lens from oxidative cloudiness and helps maintain the transparency needed for sharp focus. Vitamin E, a fat-soluble antioxidant, protects the fatty membranes of photoreceptor cells. The NIH ODS Vitamin C fact sheet confirms its unique role in ocular tissue protection.

Zinc
Ingredient 06
Visual Cycle & Retinal Pigment Support

Zinc is the most abundant trace mineral in the eye, concentrated especially in the retina and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) — the supportive cell layer underlying the photoreceptors. Zinc supports the visual cycle by enabling Vitamin A transport from the liver to the retina and assisting in rhodopsin production. The NIH ODS Zinc fact sheet and the FDA dietary supplement guidelines both confirm zinc’s critical role in visual function.
